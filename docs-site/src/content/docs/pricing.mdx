Traditional voice architectures stitch together three separate cloud services: Speech-to-Text (ASR), a Large Language Model (LLM), and Text-to-Speech (TTS). Each service bills differently—ASR bills by the audio minute, the LLM by the text token, and TTS by the character.

**Gemini Live collapses the entire pipeline into a single native speech-to-speech model**, slashing latency and enabling natural, human-like voice conversations.

To help engineering and finance teams evaluate unit economics, Google publishes both **per-million-token prices** and **per-minute audio equivalents**. Understanding how these two numbers relate—and how a live, two-way phone conversation is actually metered—is essential for accurate telephony budgeting.

## 2. How the Per-Minute Baseline Is Derived

Telecom and contact-center (CCaaS) architects budget in **Cost Per Minute (CPM)**. The official per-minute figures simply convert how fast audio turns into tokens back into clock time:

1. **Caller Audio Input (`~$0.005 / min`)**: The caller's voice streams into Gemini Live at approximately **27.8 tokens per second** (~1,667 tokens per minute):
   ```text
   1,667 tokens/min × ($3.00 / 1,000,000 tokens) = $0.005001 / minute
   ```
2. **Model Audio Output (`~$0.018 / min`)**: The bot's voice is generated at approximately **25 tokens per second** (~1,500 tokens per minute):
   ```text
   1,500 tokens/min × ($12.00 / 1,000,000 tokens) = $0.018000 / minute
   ```
3. **Continuous Streaming Baseline (`~$0.023 / min`)**: If both sides simply streamed audio non-stop with no back-and-forth at all, pure audio processing would total:
   ```text
   $0.005/min (audio in) + $0.018/min (audio out) ≈ $0.023 / minute
   ```

## 3. Why Real-World Calls Diverge: The Four Billing Forces

When teams deploy voice agents into live customer service or sales telephony, they quickly observe that real phone calls bill higher than `$0.023/min`—often landing between **$0.08 and $0.20+ per minute**.

This is not a billing error. It is the natural consequence of **four forces** in how a live, two-way session is metered:

### Force 1: Turn-Based Re-Evaluation ("The Photocopier Effect")

Gemini Live does not meter by the clock; it meters **per model turn**—each time the model has to produce an answer.

Whenever the caller finishes speaking—or talks over the bot mid-sentence (a **barge-in**)—a **new model turn** begins. On *every single turn*, the model re-reads the whole call so far, from scratch:
* Your `systemInstruction` (persona, SOP rules, guardrails)
* All declared tool JSON schemas
* The accumulated conversational history from all prior turns

In a typical 1-minute phone call with **6 back-and-forth turns** (including 1 caller barge-in), active prompt context is re-evaluated **6 times in that single minute**:

### Force 2: Modal Memory Asymmetry (Caller Audio vs. Bot Speech)

In plain terms: **the bot remembers its own words as text, but remembers the caller's words as audio.** That single difference is the most important—and least understood—part of Gemini Live billing:

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Bot Speech Collapses to Cheap Text" icon="approve-check">
    **$0.75 / 1M Tokens on Subsequent Turns**
    
    When the assistant speaks, you pay **$12.00/M** for audio generation on that turn. However, on all subsequent turns, the bot's audio is **automatically collapsed into a text transcript** (~0.25 text tokens per audio token) and re-billed at standard text input rates (**$0.75/M**).
  </Card>

  <Card title="Caller Speech Stays as Raw Audio" icon="warning">
    **$3.00 / 1M Tokens on Subsequent Turns**
    
    Unlike bot speech, **caller spoken audio remains in the context window as raw audio tokens**. On every subsequent turn, historical caller audio is re-evaluated at **$3.00/M**—making carried user speech **4&times; to 6&times; more expensive** per word than text memory.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

### Force 3: Zero Default Prompt Caching &amp; Model Scaffolding

In plain terms: **there is no "already read this" discount on a live call.** In standard request/response APIs, repeated prompts often receive automatic cache discounts. In a real-time voice session (the `BidiGenerateContent` WebSocket), the carried context is billed at full input rates on every turn (`cached_content_token_count: 0`).

Additionally, live audio models carry a fixed internal overhead (~500 tokens of model-side scaffolding per turn on `gemini-3.1-flash-live` and `gemini-3.5-flash-live`) that is billed alongside your own prompt on every turn—even if your prompt were empty.

### Force 4: The Telephony 0–5s Drop Distribution

In enterprise telephony (outbound qualification, customer support, appointment reminders), a large share of calls end almost immediately: **30% to 50% of calls terminate within the first 5 seconds** due to voicemails, wrong numbers, or instant hangups.

If an agent opens every call with one heavy 4,500-token system prompt and 8 tool declarations, **100% of that upfront prompt weight is billed on Turn 1**—even when the caller hangs up 3 seconds later.

## 5. Verify It on Your Own Traffic

You do not have to take this page's word for it. Every Gemini Live server message can carry a `usage_metadata` block. Log it once per turn and all four forces become visible in minutes:

```python
# Inside your receive loop (google-genai Live API)
async for msg in session.receive():
    usage = msg.usage_metadata
    if usage:
        by_modality = {
            d.modality: d.token_count
            for d in (usage.prompt_tokens_details or [])
        }
        log.info(
            "turn=%d prompt=%d audio_in=%d text_in=%d cached=%d out=%d",
            turn_index,
            usage.prompt_token_count,
            by_modality.get("AUDIO", 0),
            by_modality.get("TEXT", 0),
            usage.cached_content_token_count or 0,
            usage.response_token_count,
        )
```

What to look for:

| Signal in the log | What it means | Force |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `prompt` **climbs turn after turn** | You are paying the re-read tax. Each turn re-bills the previous ones. | 1 |
| `audio_in` is the fastest-growing number | The caller's raw audio is being carried in history at $3.00/M. This is the biggest lever. | 2 |
| `cached` is `0` | No caching discount is being applied. Budget every re-read at full rate. | 3 |
| `text_in` is large on turn 1, even for a 3-second call | Your opening prompt and tool schemas are heavy; dropped calls pay for all of it. | 4 |

<Aside type="tip" title="One-line health check">
  If `prompt` on turn 10 is not roughly the same as on turn 3, the session is not being trimmed and the bill is compounding. That is the one number to put on a dashboard.
</Aside>

## 6. Bridging the Gap: Can You Achieve ~$0.03 / Minute?

Once the four forces are visible, it is clear why an unoptimized voice deployment sees its per-minute cost climb as calls get longer.

**However, high multi-turn costs are entirely avoidable.** By structuring how instructions, tool schemas, and historical audio flow through your WebSocket session, engineering teams routinely eliminate the turn-compounding multiplier and bring real-world blended telephony costs right back down to **~$0.028 – $0.035 per minute**—while keeping response latency under 600ms.

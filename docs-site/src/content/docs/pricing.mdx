Most voice applications wire three separate services together: speech-to-text, an LLM, and text-to-speech. Each service meters differently. Speech-to-text charges by the audio minute, the LLM charges by text tokens, and text-to-speech charges by character.

Gemini Live replaces that chain with a single speech-to-speech model. Because audio streams straight through the model, Google quotes pricing in two ways: per-token rates and estimated per-minute audio equivalents.

Here is how the math works, why multi-turn calls bill differently than a single audio stream, and how to verify what your app is spending.

---

## 1. The official rate card

Published pricing for `gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview` across Google AI Studio and Vertex AI:

| Modality | Price per 1M tokens | Audio minute equivalent | Counted usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Caller audio input** | $3.00 | ~$0.005 / min | User audio streamed at 16 kHz PCM (~27.8 tokens/sec) |
| **Model audio output** | $12.00 | ~$0.018 / min | Assistant speech streamed at 24 kHz PCM (~25 tokens/sec) |
| **Input text** | $0.75 | N/A | System prompt, tool schemas, client text history |
| **Output text** | $4.50 | N/A | Transcripts and thinking tokens |
| **Input video / image** | $1.00 | ~$0.002 / min | Realtime video frames (~1 fps, ~258 tokens per frame) |

<Aside type="note" title="Thinking tokens">
  Output pricing includes reasoning tokens. Setting `thinkingLevel: "minimal"` on `gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview` reduces thinking token volume and cuts initial audio delay.
</Aside>

## 2. Where the per-minute numbers come from

Telephony teams budget by cost per minute. The per-minute rates on Google's rate card come directly from the token consumption rate of raw PCM audio:

1. **Caller audio input (~$0.005 / min)**: Spoken 16 kHz PCM audio converts to roughly 27.8 tokens per second, or about 1,667 tokens per minute:
   ```text
   1,667 tokens/min × ($3.00 / 1,000,000 tokens) = $0.005001 / minute
   ```
2. **Model audio output (~$0.018 / min)**: Synthesized 24 kHz PCM audio runs at roughly 25 tokens per second, or about 1,500 tokens per minute:
   ```text
   1,500 tokens/min × ($12.00 / 1,000,000 tokens) = $0.018000 / minute
   ```
3. **Continuous streaming baseline (~$0.023 / min)**: If caller and assistant spoke back and forth in one continuous stream without pauses or interruptions, audio processing alone would total:
   ```text
   $0.005/min (audio in) + $0.018/min (audio out) ≈ $0.023 / minute
   ```

## 3. Why real phone calls cost more: turns, cache, and barge-ins

When teams deploy voice agents to phone lines, real calls often run between $0.08 and $0.20 per minute instead of $0.023.

This happens because of four factors in how live audio sessions work:

### Turn-based re-evaluation
Gemini Live bills per model turn, not by wall-clock time.

Every time the caller finishes speaking, or interrupts the bot mid-sentence, the model runs a new generation turn. On every turn, the model re-evaluates the active session context:
* The system prompt (persona, business rules, guardrails)
* Tool JSON schemas
* Conversation history from earlier turns

### Modal memory asymmetry: caller audio vs. bot speech
The model remembers its own speech as text, but remembers the caller's speech as audio.

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Bot speech becomes cheap text" icon="approve-check">
    **$0.75 / 1M tokens on later turns**
    
    When the assistant speaks, you pay $12.00/M for generated audio. On later turns, that assistant audio collapses into a text transcript (~0.25 text tokens per audio token) and is re-read at standard text input rates ($0.75/M).
  </Card>

  <Card title="Caller speech stays as raw audio" icon="warning">
    **$3.00 / 1M tokens on later turns**
    
    Caller audio stays in context as raw audio tokens. On every later turn, that audio is re-evaluated at $3.00/M. That makes carried caller audio four to six times more expensive per word than bot text.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

### Zero default prompt caching and model scaffolding
Live voice sessions on the WebSocket protocol do not receive automatic caching discounts. Carried context is billed at full input rates on every turn (`cached_content_token_count: 0`).

Live models also include around 500 tokens of internal scaffolding per turn that is billed alongside your prompt, even if your prompt is empty.

### Short call drop distribution
In phone systems, 30% to 50% of calls end within five seconds due to voicemails, wrong numbers, or immediate hangups.

If an agent loads a 4,500-token prompt and eight tools when connecting, you pay for the full prompt on turn 1 even if the caller hangs up three seconds later.

Longer calls widen this difference because re-read costs grow with every turn of history, while an optimized pipeline keeps per-turn tokens steady.

## 5. Verify usage on your own traffic

Every Gemini Live server message can include a `usage_metadata` object. Logging it per turn shows these dynamics directly:

```python
# Inside your receive loop (google-genai Live API)
async for msg in session.receive():
    usage = msg.usage_metadata
    if usage:
        by_modality = {
            d.modality: d.token_count
            for d in (usage.prompt_tokens_details or [])
        }
        log.info(
            "turn=%d prompt=%d audio_in=%d text_in=%d cached=%d out=%d",
            turn_index,
            usage.prompt_token_count,
            by_modality.get("AUDIO", 0),
            by_modality.get("TEXT", 0),
            usage.cached_content_token_count or 0,
            usage.response_token_count,
        )
```

Key signals to check:

| Metric | What it means | Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `prompt` climbs each turn | Each turn re-reads and bills previous turns. | 1 |
| `audio_in` grows fastest | Caller raw audio is carried in history at $3.00/M. | 2 |
| `cached` is 0 | No caching discount applies; every re-read pays full rate. | 3 |
| `text_in` is high on turn 1 | Opening prompt and tools are heavy; short calls pay full price. | 4 |

<Aside type="tip" title="Health check">
  If `prompt` on turn 10 is noticeably larger than on turn 3, older turns are accumulating in context and inflating the per-minute rate.
</Aside>

## 6. Keeping costs near the rate card target

Unmanaged voice sessions become expensive because context compounds over time.

You can keep call costs between $0.028 and $0.035 per minute by managing what stays in context:
1. Start with small prompt cards (~120 tokens) so short dropped calls do not pay for unused instructions.
2. Cap audio history with 5,000-token compression so old caller audio does not get re-evaluated indefinitely.
3. Convert older caller audio to text transcripts after turn 3, saving roughly 83% on stored history.
